## 1. Executive Summary & Curriculum Philosophy

### The App is a Primary Course; The Book is a Reference
The **Learn English: Grammar Games** Intermediate Course is an independent, interactive digital learning curriculum engineered for learners navigating the critical transition from foundational fluency (B1) to sophisticated control and expressive precision (B2). While traditional grammar handbooks arrange units alphabetically or morphologically, our curriculum is structured according to **cognitive progression**, **functional discourse**, and **contrastive grammar mechanics**.

### Copyright & Plagiarism Strict Ban
**Zero-Copyright Infringement Guarantee**: None of the explanations, communicative examples, guided drills, interactive mini-games, or test questions in our course are copied from Raymond Murphy's *English Grammar in Use*. The book serves exclusively as an external reference standard for syllabus benchmarking, level alignment, and study companion indexing.

### The B1–B2 Intermediate Pedagogy: Meaning, Choice, and Pragmatics
At the Beginner level (A1–A2), learners primarily acquire structural forms (*What is the formula?*). At the Intermediate level (B1–B2), grammar shifts fundamentally into **Choice and Pragmatics**:
1. **Which form is correct here?** — Discerning subtle grammatical differences in identical situations.
2. **Why?** — Understanding the speaker's perspective, stance, and communicative intent.
3. **What changes the meaning?** — Stative vs dynamic verbs, simple vs continuous aspect, open vs closed time frames, degrees of modal certainty, and defining vs non-defining relative information.
4. **Discourse Cohesion** — Mastering complex clause linking, fronting, passive voice for focus, and idiomatic phrasal combinations.

## 2. Curriculum Macro-Phases

The 18 sections of the Intermediate Course are organized into 4 strategic progression phases and 1 capstone mastery challenge:

* **PHASE 1 — Tense Control (Sections 01–03)**
  * Section 01: Present and Past Review (Dynamic vs Stative, Habitual vs Temporary)
  * Section 02: Present Perfect and Past (Open vs Finished Time, Duration, Narrative Past)
  * Section 03: The Future (Intentions, Arrangements, Timetables, Predictions & Future In The Past)
* **PHASE 2 — Meaning and Choice (Sections 04–07)**
  * Section 04: Modal Verbs (Ability, Obligation, Deduction, Advice, Permission, Habit)
  * Section 05: Conditionals and Wish (Unreal Present, Past Regrets, Mixed Scenarios)
  * Section 06: The Passive Voice (Focus, Impersonal Reporting, Causative Have/Get)
  * Section 07: Reported Speech (Backshift, Reporting Verbs, Speech Act Pragmatics)
* **PHASE 3 — Building Complex Sentences (Sections 08–12)**
  * Section 08: Questions and Auxiliary Verbs (Echo Questions, Tags, Indirect Questions)
  * Section 09: Verb Patterns, Gerunds and Infinitives (Verb Complementation, Meaning Shifts)
  * Section 10: Articles and Nouns (Specific vs General, Uncountable/Countable Nuances)
  * Section 11: Pronouns and Determiners (Quantifiers, Distributives, Generic Pronouns)
  * Section 12: Relative Clauses (Defining vs Non-Defining, Reduced Participle Clauses)
* **PHASE 4 — Precision and Connection (Sections 13–17)**
  * Section 13: Adjectives and Adverbs (Gradable vs Non-gradable, Adverbial Position, Nuance)
  * Section 14: Word Order and Focus (Fronting, Inversion, Cleft Sentences, Emphasis)
  * Section 15: Conjunctions and Linking Words (Contrast, Concession, Purpose, Result, Reason)
  * Section 16: Prepositions (Time, Place, Dependent Prepositions, Prepositional Collocations)
  * Section 17: Phrasal Verbs (Separability, Idiomatic Extensions, Particle Networks)
* **CAPSTONE — Final Mastery (Section 18)**
  * Section 18: Intermediate Final Challenge (Mastery Sprints, Diagnostic Checkpoints, Error Detective)

## 1. Executive Summary & Curriculum Philosophy
### The App is a Primary Course; The Book is a Reference Standard
The **Learn English: Grammar Games** Advanced Course is an independent, interactive digital learning curriculum engineered for learners transitioning from upper-intermediate competence (B2) to advanced stylistic precision, structural flexibility, and discourse control (C1). While Martin Hewings' *Advanced Grammar in Use* serves as our external syllabus benchmark, our app is not a digitized book: it is an active, data-driven learning engine with cognitive progression, game-based learning mechanics, and deep diagnostic analytics.

### Strict Plagiarism & Copyright Guarantee
None of the explanations, pedagogical descriptions, rules, examples, or interactive questions are copied from *Advanced Grammar in Use*. All learning content is 100% original, authored specifically for communicative interaction, grammatical awareness, and game mechanics.

### The C1 Advanced Pedagogy: Nuance, Precision, and Rhetorical Focus
Advanced grammar is **not** 'Intermediate + more difficult vocabulary'. At C1, the learner already understands all basic and intermediate structural formulas. The true challenges of C1 revolve around **communicative intent, register sensitivity, and information packaging**:
1. **Why choose this structure over that one?** — Discerning subtle communicative differences between two grammatically valid constructions (e.g. *I didn't need to go* vs *I needn't have gone*; *If it were to happen* vs *Should it happen*).
2. **Information Structure & Thematic Focus** — Shaping clauses for end-weight, topic continuity, psychological proximity, or dramatic emphasis (*It-clefts*, *Wh-clefts*, *Locative Inversion*, *Negative Fronting*).
3. **Register & Stylistic Flexibility** — Switching between informal spoken discourse and formal academic/legal prose (*Putative should*, *Mandative subjunctive*, *Pied-piping preposition fronting*).
4. **Economical Synthesis** — Compressing complex subordinate clauses into non-finite participle adjuncts and nominalized structures.

## 2. Curriculum Macro-Phases

The 20 sections of the Advanced Course are structured across 6 pedagogical phases:

* **PHASE 1 — Time and Perspective (Sections 01–02)**
  * Section 01: Advanced Tense Contrasts (Aspectual nuance, stative flexibility, retrospective sequence)
  * Section 02: Advanced Future Forms (Impersonal schedules, be to / be about to, past perspectives on future)
* **PHASE 2 — Meaning and Stance (Sections 03–05)**
  * Section 03: Modals and Semi-Modals (Counterfactuals, specific achievement, fine modal idioms)
  * Section 04: Linking Verbs and Complex Predication (Copular verbs, sensory perception, change of state)
  * Section 05: Advanced Passive (Discourse flow, end-weight, impersonal reporting frames)
* **PHASE 3 — Complex Sentence Structures (Sections 06–09)**
  * Section 06: Advanced Questions (Preposition fronting, negative/echo questions, embedded that-clauses)
  * Section 07: Verb Complementation (Ergative pairs, ditransitive shifts, gerund vs infinitive semantics)
  * Section 08: Advanced Reporting (Summarizing verbs, backshift retention, mandative subjunctive)
  * Section 09: Agreement and Complex Noun Phrases (Complex concord, collective nouns, compound nominals)
* **PHASE 4 — Complex Noun and Clause Systems (Sections 10–12)**
  * Section 10: Advanced Articles and Determiners (Generic classes, institutional zero article, polarity)
  * Section 11: Relative and Participle Clauses (Pied-piping, whereby/wherein, non-finite participle adjuncts)
  * Section 12: Pronouns, Substitution and Ellipsis (So/not clausal substitutes, do so, auxiliary reduction)
* **PHASE 5 — Precision and Connection (Sections 13–17)**
  * Section 13: Advanced Adjectives and Adverbs (Gradability, collocations, viewpoint adverbs, scope)
  * Section 14: Adverbial Clauses and Linking (Concession vs condition, purposeful clauses, text cohesion)
  * Section 15: Advanced Conditionals (Mixed hypotheses, inverted conditionals with were/had/should)
  * Section 16: Advanced Prepositions (Topological metaphors, exception markers, dependent collocations)
  * Section 17: Multi-word Verb Structures (Particle placement, three-part verbs, pronominal syntax)
* **PHASE 6 — Information and Emphasis (Sections 18–20)**
  * Section 18: Organising Information (Existential there, dummy/anticipatory it, cleft focus structures)
  * Section 19: Focus and Inversion (Negative adverbial fronting, locative inversion, emphatic operators)
  * Section 20: Advanced Final Challenge (Integrative C1 mastery capstone)

| Grammar Domain | Beginner (A1–A2) Depth | Intermediate (B1–B2) Depth | Advanced (C1) Depth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Past & Perfect** | `FOUNDATION` (Simple past vs have done basics) | `CONTRAST` (Open vs Finished time, duration) | `MASTERY` (Psychological proximity, narrative framing, retrospective sequence) |
| **Future Forms** | `FOUNDATION` (Will vs going to) | `CONTRAST` (Arrangements vs Intentions) | `NUANCE` / `MASTERY` (Be to, be about to, future in the past, prospective destiny) |
